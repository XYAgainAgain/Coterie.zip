# How to ***Coterie***

This quick little guide should tell you most of what you need to know for the night-to-night action of ***Coterie***. If it isn't covered here, it's probably covered in the rules on the [main site](https://coterie.zip/). Search or ask your Storyteller!

## Rolling Dice

Just about everything resolves by rolling **2d6 + Stat**. Describe what your character does; if it triggers a Move, the Storyteller will ask you to roll. Results fall into five tiers, from worst to best:

- **Bestial Failure** = all 1s on every die you rolled (2d6 or more). Something primal & ugly happens: you gain a point of Hunger, the Beast surfaces in a way you can't hide, or worse. Think of it as a critical failure with fangs.
- **6-** = Failure; the Storyteller makes a Move against you (things get worse, never "nothing happens")
- **7–9** = Mixed Success; you get what you wanted, but with a cost, complication, or hard choice
- **10+** = Success; you get what you wanted, nice & clean, good job!
- **12+** = Critical Success; many Discipline Powers and other abilities include a 12+ result by default. Basic Moves only get one if you've purchased the Advanced upgrade for that specific Move.

**Advantage** means roll 3d6 and ignore the lowest die roll. **Disadvantage** means roll 3d6 and ignore the highest die roll. Multiple sources of either cancel one another out (you still only ever roll 3d6), meaning if you have one or more of each from any source, you roll "flat" (just 2d6).

**+X or −X Forward** is a one-time bonus or penalty to your next relevant roll, then it's gone.

**+X or −X Ongoing** is a persistent bonus/penalty that lasts until the condition causing it ends.

**Hold** is a currency you bank from a roll and spend later, usually 1-for-1 on a specific list of options. Critically, Hold *cannot* be spent across Moves or Powers; think of each point of Hold like a token you can exchange with whatever generated it to cause a specific effect or get you some information.

## Your Stats

Five stats define your character. You'll roll with whichever one fits the fiction. All Moves and Powers will say which to roll. Some will tell you to use the higher of two stats, and some (like **Influence**) change which stat is used depending on your approach (so your Storyteller might say "Roll **Influence** plus Shadow").

- **Blood** = primal instinct, predatory awareness, connection to the Beast and other vampires
- **Shadow** = stealth, subterfuge, deception, operating unseen/unheard/unnoticed
- **Resolve** = composure, willpower, physical/emotional resilience, standing your ground
- **Wits** = perception, quick thinking, investigation, knowledge, reading a situation
- **Demeanor** = social presence, charm, charisma, commanding attention

These stats are used for almost everything in the game, and whenever you roll for one of the Basic Moves (with 1 exception), you'll add your modifier. The 12 Basic Moves are **Influence**, **Discern Vibes**, **Catch the Scent**, **Help or Hinder**, **Stay Chill**, **Slip Away**, **Reposition**, **Dirty Your Claws**, **Hunt**, **Feed**, **Protect the Coterie**, and **Blush of Life** (the exception; this one only requires a Hunger Check).

## Your Coterie

This is your tight-knit group, crew, squad, gang, family, flock, or pack of other vampires like you. You share a Haven (safe territory) and must work together to get things done. Other members of your Coterie are almost exclusively player characters (PCs), but you might have some non-player characters (NPCs) in there too.

The Coterie has its own little "character sheet" with another 5 stats (**Clout**, **Cohesion**, **Charm**, **Claim**, & **Currency**) that each have a minimum of −3 and maximum of +3. When you use Coterie Moves (whole-group Moves, each usable once per night), each will tell you what stat to roll with, and you'll always add your Coterie's Cohesion stat to those. Your Coterie's stats are expected to go up and down during play.

## XP

Your character is not static despite technically being dead. Each Playbook comes with 3 XP triggers, each granting +1 XP per night if the conditions are met. Some Moves, such as **Help or Hinder**, can also grant XP (with limits). You might also be granted **Storyteller Awards** of a few XP when your Coterie accomplishes something important or overcame a challenge.

Additionally, whenever your Coterie rests, the Storyteller will ask the group 3 Yes/No questions, called **Slumber Reflections**:

1. Did your Coterie put themselves at risk to protect or advance something you care about?
2. Did your Coterie learn a secret, uncover a truth, reveal/solve a mystery, or otherwise find something important?
3. Did a relationship or connection between any Coterie members or major allies change significantly?

If 1 = Yes, no XP is gained. If 2 = Yes, everyone gains +1 XP. If all 3 = Yes, everyone gains +2 XP instead.

**XP Penalty:** Characters with BP 3 or higher reduce the XP they can gain from **Storyteller Awards** or **Slumber Reflections**; the first two methods (**Playbook Triggers** & **Moves That Say So**) are unaffected.

## Hunger

Hunger is tracked from 0 (fully sated) to 5 (starving & dangerous). It goes up when you wake from slumber, push your limits, use certain Discipline Powers, or just have bad luck. It goes down when you **Feed**.

**Hunger Checks** are rolled whenever you activate a Discipline Power or Blood Surge: roll 1d6. If the result is equal to or lower than your current Hunger, gain +1 Hunger. If it's higher, nothing happens.

- At **3 Hunger**, you take −1 Ongoing to all rolls.
- At **4 Hunger**, that worsens to −2 Ongoing.
- At **5 Hunger**, you are on the edge of Frenzy: you must **Stay Chill** at the start of each of your turns (and/or whenever the Storyteller deems appropriate) or lose control entirely.

When you **Frenzy**, you're still playing your character, but you *must* play the Frenzy honestly. The Beast chows down, brutally fights, or flees with no regard for consequences, and you don't get to soften it. Frenzy is terrifying, messy, and almost always a Masquerade violation if spotted. Don't let your Hunger get to 5!

## Feeding

Getting the yummy red stuff (or your personal equivalent) is a two-step process: **Hunt** is how you find prey, and **Feed** is how you drink from it. Both are Basic Moves with their own triggers and outcomes. **Feeding** reduces your Hunger, but the amount depends on the source, your roll, and your Predator Type.

Your Blood Potency restricts what can sustain you:

- **BP 0–1:** No restrictions; animals, blood bags, and mortals all work
- **BP 2:** Animals and blood bags reduce Hunger by 1 less (unless something says otherwise)
- **BP 3:** Animal blood provides no sustenance at all (unless something says otherwise)
- **BP 4:** Below 2 Hunger, you must drain a mortal to (or past) the brink of death
- **BP 5:** Below 3 Hunger, you must drain a mortal completely; reaching 0 Hunger always means someone dies

## Humanity

Humanity is a 10-to-0 scale tracking how much of "you" is left versus how much belongs to the Beast. At 0, your character becomes a Wight, a mindless predator under the Storyteller's permanent control. You'll have to retire them and build someone new. Many things scale with Humanity, but the one you'll likely encounter most frequently is the **Blush of Life** Basic Move, which has a clear breakdown of what it does.

**Convictions** are absolute moral beliefs ("Always protect the vulnerable" or "Never break your word"). You should have at least 2. **Touchstones** are living mortals who embody those Convictions, one per Conviction. Protecting and fostering these relationships is how you hold onto your Humanity.

**Stains** mark your Humanity track when you violate your Convictions or commit atrocities. Minor violations earn 1 Stain; serious ones earn more. Acting in direct service of a Conviction can remove Stains. If you accumulate 5 or more Stains (or fill your remaining Humanity track, if it's shorter than 5), you automatically lose 1 Humanity and leftover Stains roll over.

A **Remorse Check** happens at the end of each night when you climb into your coffin: roll 1d6 and try to roll over your current Stain count. Success clears all Stains and doesn't reduce your Humanity (good). Failure also clears all Stains, but reduces your Humanity by 1 (bad).

Low Humanity has serious penalties. At **3 Humanity**, take −1 Ongoing to social rolls with mortals and −1 Ongoing to **Stay Chill**. At **2 Humanity**, both worsen to −2. At **1 Humanity**, both worsen to −3 and you lose all remaining Touchstones and Convictions. If your character is a Baali, you already know all of this.

## Blood Potency

Blood Potency (0–5) is the double-edged sword of vampiric existence. Higher isn't necessarily better, but it sure is stronger. It scales and interacts with almost everything:

- **Harm Capacity:** 6 HP at BP 0–1, then +3 per BP level (9 → 12 → 15 → 18 HP).
- **Blood Surges:** Activate with a Hunger Check whenever you want to gain a number of Advantages equal to your BP to use on rolls within that scene (they don't have to be consecutive). Unused Advantages expire at scene's end or slumber. You can Blood Surge BP times per night.
- **Bane Severity:** Your Bane's penalties scale with your BP (always minimum 1).
- **Compulsion Intensity:** The Ongoing penalty from a triggered Compulsion equals your BP.
- **Discipline Powers:** You can only learn or use Powers of a level up to your current BP.
- **Feeding Restrictions:** Higher BP demands fresher, more dangerous sources (see above).
- **Advancement Penalty:** At BP 3, −1 XP from Slumber Reflections and Storyteller Awards. At BP 4–5, −2 XP from those sources. XP you get from Moves (such as **Help or Hinder**) or from your Playbook's triggers aren't affected.

## Harm

Characters in ***Coterie*** can take two types of damage: 

1. **Superficial Harm** comes from mundane violence (bullets, blades, falls, forklifts, rolled-up newspapers, bad jokes, basically everything that might hurt a human) and can be healed quickly.
2. **Aggravated Harm** comes from fire, sunlight, and supernatural sources, and does not heal as easily.

Your **Harm Track** has HP equal to your Blood Potency tier (6/9/12/15/18). Mark Superficial Harm with a slash; mark Aggravated Harm by filling the box completely.

When your Harm Track fills entirely with a **majority** of Superficial Harm, you reach 0 HP and enter **Torpor**, a deathlike stasis. You're helpless and vulnerable until someone gives you some blood or enough time passes that your body is able to start patching itself up.

When your Harm Track contains **half or more (rounded up)** Aggravated Harm and you reach 0 HP, that's **Final Death**. No coming back from that one (*or is there...?*), sorry pal.

**Healing:** At the beginning or end of any scene while you have Superficial Harm, you can choose to make a Hunger Check. Regardless of whether your Hunger increases, you heal Superficial Harm equal to your BP (minimum 1). Slumbering after **Feeding** at least once that night lets you heal all Superficial Harm on waking. Healing Aggravated Harm can only happen via slumber if your Hunger is 2 or lower when you go to bed, clearing 1 + your BP Aggravated Harm. Otherwise, it takes Powers, **Feeding**, or other abilities to heal.

## Combat & Initiative

When things get chaotic, ***Coterie*** shifts from a free-form game to using a structured Initiative list. When asked to roll Initiative, roll **2d6 + Wits or Blood** (whichever is higher) and note down your number. The spotlight alternates between player characters and opponents or the environment, each taking a turn as the fiction demands until you reach the bottom of the order. Then you begin again at the top, starting a new round. **Each round of combat lasts for 5 fictional seconds.**

On your turn in combat, you can do one of each of the following:

1. **Movement:** Narrative positioning in physical space; you can travel up to one Range, from **Distant ↔ Far ↔ Close ↔ Hand ↔ Intimate**
2. **Action:** Any Basic Move or Discipline Power that doesn't say it takes a different part of the action economy (some do). *Remember, you must narrate your character's actual actions to trigger your Moves!*
3. **Free Action:** Basic things your character can do without expending any extra effort or time while performing other tasks. A good rule of thumb is if you (the player) could safely and easily perform the task while actively cooking a meal or driving a vehicle, it can count as a Free Action.

Initiative isn't only used for fighting! Your Storyteller may opt to enter Initiative when fast reactions are needed, such as during a chase (in either direction), when something explodes and you need to leave NOW, or in high-stakes (heh) social situations.

**Getting Staked:** If you get staked (meaning someone jams something sharp into your heart), you are immediately *incapacitated*. You can't move, speak, use Disciplines, or take any action whatsoever (unless you have something that says you can or have somehow protected yourself from getting staked). You're conscious and aware of your surroundings, but paralyzed. Communicating telepathically works fine, if you can do that.

**Staking Someone Else:** Staking in combat requires a declaration of your intent, an opponent genuinely powerless to stop it, and a successful **Dirty Your Claws** roll with a weapon that could plausibly reach the heart. **On a 10+,** the stake lands and the target is incapacitated. **On a 7–9,** the Storyteller chooses whether the stake finds its mark or merely deals normal Harm. **On a 6-,** the attempt fails entirely and possibly backfires.

## Disciplines

These are broad categories of vampiric abilities, and each comes with a Perk you get for free when you acquire access. Your Playbook gives you starting access to at least 1–2, selected during character creation. Your Predator Type may also give you another (if duplicate, this grants you 1 free Discipline Power instead).

There is no limit to the number of Disciplines most vampiric characters can access, but you cannot unlock or use Discipline Powers of a level higher than your Blood Potency (unless you're a Thin-Blood, but they've got their own thing going on). There are 4 types of Discipline Powers in ***Coterie***:

1. **Discipline Perks** = granted for free when you unlock a Discipline, usually no rolls required
2. **Passive Powers** = purchased with XP, provide permission to do cool stuff, usually no rolls required
3. **Active Powers** = purchased with XP, almost always require rolls + Hunger Checks
4. **Project Powers** = purchased with XP, big effects, take time (sometimes Clocks), usually gnarly

## Debt

**Debts** are the social currency of vampiric society, representing favors owed, wrongs forgiven, and leverage held. They're tracked between individual characters and between your Coterie and the world.

**Invoke** a Debt for +2 Forward on a social or investigative roll against someone who owes you. The fiction must justify it. Once per Debt per night; doesn't erase the Debt.

**Cash In** a Debt to demand a concrete favor: honest answers, physical backup, access to resources, cover stories, staying out of your way, etc. One Debt = one favor. This erases the Debt.

**Refuse** to honor a Debt you owe, and the Coterie instantly loses 1 Clout. The Storyteller also picks a complication: mark 1 Stain, make an enemy, or the creditor decides you still owe them and escalates.

**Soft Cap:** 5 personal Debts in either direction, 3 Coterie Debts in either direction. Past that, you're drowning and should seek assistance.

## Clocks

**Clocks** are long-term trackers of (you guessed it) time and effort. They come in 4, 6, and 8-segment varieties, and are most often used to track the progress of larger things in the background. Long-term projects, along with some Rituals, Ceremonies, Sacraments, and Formulae all require Clock usage.

**The Masquerade Clock** is the one you should be worried about. It's tracked per-Coterie and can get you staked, killed, or worse. The local vampiric authorities are always watching. You should try not to breach the Masquerade by using obvious Discipline Powers, drinking blood in public where humans can see, or doing anything else to expose the existence of vampires to the general populace.

If you do, expect swift retribution once all 8 of its segments are filled. Many Discipline Powers are obvious Masquerade breaches; they will say so. If they don't, be careful anyway.
